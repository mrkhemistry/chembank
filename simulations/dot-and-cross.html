    What is a dative bond?

    A dative (coordinate) bond is a covalent bond where both shared electrons come from the same atom. Once formed it is identical to any other covalent bond; the arrow only records where the pair came from.

    How to spot one

    Count the central atom's outer electrons. If it ends up forming more bonds than it has electrons to share, such as nitrogen with four bonds, one of those bonds must be dative.

    Drawing the arrow

    Point the arrow from the donor (the atom giving the lone pair) to the acceptor: N to H in NH4+, O to C in CO, N to O in the nitrogen oxides. Both electrons of that pair carry the donor's symbol.

    Ions and charges

    For an ion, draw the whole species in square brackets and put the overall charge outside, top-right. In an ionic solid such as CaSO4, each ion gets its own brackets and the metal ion has an empty outer shell.
